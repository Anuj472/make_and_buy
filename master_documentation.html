Make-or-Buy Decision Scenarios

Comprehensive framework for Multiple Criteria Decision Making (MCDM)

🎯

Scenario 1: Maximize Strategic Competitive Performance

Goal: Make decisions that help the company win market share by offering the best product/service.

Data Attribute Description Data Type
Delivery Lead Time How fast the vendor can deliver vs. internal production time. Days (Numeric)
Delivery Reliability Historical on-time delivery rate. Percentage (%)
Defect Rate / Yield Internal factory yield rate vs. vendor's quality defect rate. Percentage (%)
Volume Flexibility Minimum Order Quantities (MOQ) and speed of scaling production. Score (-8 to +8)
Core Competency Flag Is this item a core differentiator for our brand? Boolean (True/False)

🔄 How to get these data points:

  • ERP/MRP Systems: (e.g., SAP, Oracle) automatically track historical delivery times, defect rates, and on-time percentages for both internal factory and external vendors.
  • PLM (Product Lifecycle Management): Engineering teams define which parts are core IP (Core Competency Flags).
  • Procurement Contracts: Vendor MOQs and flexibility terms are extracted directly from supplier agreements.
👥

Scenario 2: Maximize Managerial Performance

Goal: Optimize internal operations, workforce health, and ease of relationship management.

Data Attribute Description Data Type
Labor Utilization / Capacity Are internal workers idle, or already exhausted by overtime? Percentage (%)
Transaction Complexity Difficulty of logistics, customs, and contract management for this option. Score (-8 to +8)
Supplier Relationship Value Strategic necessity of feeding orders to a specific vendor to maintain partnership. Score (-8 to +8)
Customer Perception Will customers react poorly to outsourcing (e.g., loss of "Made in USA" tag)? Score (-8 to +8)

🔄 How to get these data points:

  • MES (Manufacturing Execution Systems) / HR: Tracks real-time machine and worker utilization to prevent burnout.
  • Managerial Surveys (AHP): Transaction complexity and supplier relationship scores are usually gathered quarterly via internal surveys from Procurement Managers.
  • CRM / Sales Feedback: Customer perception scores are sourced from marketing and sales teams based on brand strategy.
🛡️

Scenario 3: Minimize Sourcing Risks

Goal: Protect the company from supply chain collapse, IP theft, or vendor bankruptcy.

Data Attribute Description Data Type
Intellectual Property (IP) Risk Risk of proprietary knowledge leaking to the vendor. Score (-8 to +8)
Supply Disruption Risk Vendor's financial stability, geopolitical risk, or natural disaster exposure. Score (-8 to +8)
Appropriation Risk Probability the vendor steals the design to become a direct competitor. Score (-8 to +8)
Dual-Sourcing Availability Are there alternative vendors, or does this create a monopoly dependency? Boolean (True/False)

🔄 How to get these data points:

  • Legal & Compliance Teams: Evaluate and score IP and Appropriation risks based on NDA strength and patent protection.
  • Financial Audits / Third-Party Data: Companies use services like Dun & Bradstreet to automatically pull financial solvency and geopolitical risk scores for suppliers.
  • Procurement Database: Sourcing systems flag whether an item has approved secondary suppliers (Dual-Sourcing).
💰

Scenario 4: Maximize Financial Performance

Goal: The classic approach—find the path with the absolute lowest cost and best cash flow.

Data Attribute Description Data Type
Internal Total Cost Raw Material Cost + Labor Cost + Machine Overhead. Currency (₹)
Vendor Total Cost Vendor Purchase Price + Shipping/Tariffs + Incoming QA Cost. Currency (₹)

🔄 How to get these data points:

  • Cost Accounting (ERP): Activity-Based Costing (ABC) modules provide exact internal machine overhead and labor rates.
  • Vendor RFQs: Live quotes and shipping contracts provide exact external costs.
  • Finance Department: Provides exact CapEx budgets and standardized inventory holding cost percentages.

Technical Documentation

Mathematical Objective Functions and Database Definitions used in the MILP Solver.

PART 1: The Optimization Formulas (app.py)

Since the PuLP solver is an LpMinimize engine, it always attempts to find the path with the lowest overall "Penalty" score while respecting machine capacity limits. The objective function is calculated as follows based on the selected goal:

💰 1. Maximize Financial Performance

The solver minimizes the pure financial cost (in Rupees) of acquiring the parts.

make_penalty = (raw_material_cost * (1 + expected_scrap)) + labor_cost

buy_penalty = vendor_purchase_price + incoming_qa_cost

Note: If the Financial goal is chosen, the solver ALSO adds `(machine_setup_hours + machine_run_hours) * machine_operating_cost` to the make_penalty.

🛡️ 2. Minimize Sourcing Risks

Risk scores are rated from -8 (Bad) to +8 (Good). Since the solver wants to minimize a penalty, we subtract the score from 24 (the max possible score) to create a massive positive penalty for bad risks.

risk_score = ip_leakage_risk + supplier_disruption_risk + appropriation_risk

make_penalty = 0 // Zero external risk for making it internally
buy_penalty = 24 - risk_score

👥 3. Maximize Managerial Performance

Similar to Risks, Managerial scores range from -8 (Headache) to +8 (Smooth). The solver penalizes options that cause logistical or relational headaches.

mgr_score = transaction_complexity + supplier_rel_value + customer_perception

make_penalty = 0
buy_penalty = 24 - mgr_score

🏆 4. Maximize Strategic Competitive Performance

This goal minimizes the negative impact on the customer by heavily penalizing long lead times and high defect rates. Defect rates (e.g. 0.05) are multiplied by 1000 so they mathematically weigh against lead times (e.g. 45 days).

make_penalty = internal_lead_time_days + (internal_defect_rate * 1000) - volume_flexibility_score

buy_penalty = vendor_lead_time_days + (vendor_defect_rate * 1000)

Vendor Reliability Score System

The Vendor Reliability Score (VRS) transforms qualitative supplier risk into a quantitative metric used by the MILP optimizer to adjust effective procurement costs.

VRS = (W₁ × Quality) + (W₂ × Delivery) + (W₃ × Cost) + (W₄ × Responsiveness)

The final score ranges from 0 to 1. In the optimization engine, the baseline purchase cost is divided by the VRS, dynamically increasing the effective cost of dealing with unreliable vendors.

W₁ = 0.40

🛡️ Quality Score

Measures the historical defect rate of incoming parts from the vendor.

Calculation:
(Total Units Accepted) / (Total Units Supplied)
W₂ = 0.30

⏱️ Delivery Score

Measures adherence to promised lead times to prevent production stalls.

Calculation:
(Orders Delivered On-Time) / (Total Orders Placed)
W₃ = 0.20

💵 Cost Consistency

Evaluates the supplier's tendency to suddenly escalate agreed prices.

Calculation:
1 - (Price Escalation Instances / Total Orders)
W₄ = 0.10

💬 Responsiveness

Scores the supplier's agility in communication and issue resolution.

Calculation:
1 - (Avg Response Time / Max Acceptable Time)
