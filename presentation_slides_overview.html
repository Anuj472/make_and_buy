Slide 2: Executive Summary — The Industrial Challenge & Solution SLIDE 02

The Challenge: Local Optimization Traps

  • Siloed Planning Fails: Solving Make-vs-Buy on annual machine capacity creates unexecutable weekly floor blockages.
  • Physical Coupling: CNC machines cannot run without parts wheeled from stores, tooling fixtures, and operators.
  • Bottleneck Propagation: When intermediate staging buffers fill, upstream machines are blocked from unloading.
  • Factory Reality: Mechanical shops do not have automated AGVs or IoT streaming telemetry. The system must work with manual carts and supervisor shift feedback.

The Integrated Solution Architecture

  • Pillar 1: Implemented Work (Active Codebase): Order-Level Make vs Buy MILP (PuLP), MCDM Multi-Criteria Goals, and Baseline CP-SAT Job Shop.
  • Pillar 2: In-Planning Target (Whole Factory): Flexible Job Shop routing across 19 parallel machines, sequence-dependent changeovers, inter-bay cart transfer times, and vacuum chamber batching.
  • Closed-Loop Disruption Repair: One-Click Right-Shift Repair and local re-optimization for supervisor control.
Slide 3: Part I (Implemented) — Make vs. Buy Multi-Criteria Optimizer SLIDE 03

Mathematical Formulation

  • PuLP MILP Solver: Integer variables for Make and Buy quantities.
  • Strict Demand: Make + Buy == Order_Quantity.
  • Capacity Limits: Sum of setup + run hours with scrap factors ≤ net available machine hours.
  • Strategic Lock: Critical items strictly forbidden from outsourcing (Buy == 0).

4 Selectable MCDM Goals

  • 💰 Financial: Minimizes material, labor, machine runtime, and external purchase costs.
  • 🛡️ Sourcing Risk: Penalizes IP leakage, vendor financial risk, and disruption likelihood.
  • 👥 Managerial: Optimizes transaction complexity and supplier relationship value.
  • 🏆 Strategic: Minimizes lead time and vendor defect rates.

Interactive Dashboard

  • Decision Output: Automated MAKE, BUY, or MAKE & BUY tagging.
  • Metric Cards: Instant readouts for Made vs Bought units, penalty scores, and split orders.
  • Capacity Multiplier: Slider (0.1x to 2.0x) to stress-test capacity shocks.
  • CSV Export: Outputs directly to Final_Make_Buy_Decisions.csv.
Slide 4: Part I (Implemented) — Baseline Job Shop Scheduler (CP-SAT) SLIDE 04

Google OR-Tools CP-SAT Model

  • Seamless Ingestion: Automatically filters in-house manufactured orders (Make_In_House_Qty > 0).
  • Interval Variables: Integer start, duration, and end times in minutes.
  • Disjunctive Constraints: AddNoOverlap ensures machines process only one task at a time.
  • Precedence Chains: Operation sequence strictly enforced per item routing.
  • Optimization Modes: Minimize Makespan ($C_{\max}$) or Total Due-Date Tardiness.

Working Calendar & UI Gantt

  • Real Calendar Logic: Maps minutes into 10-hour working days (8 AM - 6 PM), 6 days/week (Mon-Sat, Sun closed).
  • Interactive Gantt: Color-coded by Job ID using Plotly Timeline.
  • Cascade Filters: Filter schedule by Machine → Month → ISO Week.
  • CSV Downloads: Clean timetable export with exact Start/Finish timestamps for machine operators.

Slide 7: In-Planning Phase 1 — Flexible Job Shop (FJSSP) & SDST SLIDE 07

1. Parallel Machine Routing (FJSSP)

  • Unlocking 19 Parallel Machines: Dynamically routes jobs across 5 CNC Mills, 5 Lens Polishers, 5 Coating Chambers, and 5 Assembly Benches.
  • CP-SAT Optional Intervals: Uses AddOptionalIntervalVar with boolean presence flags x_ijk.
  • Dynamic Load Balancing: Eliminates artificial bottlenecks and reduces total makespan by 40–60%.

2. Sequence-Dependent Setup Times (SDST)

  • Industrial Changeover Matrix: S[k, family_A, family_B] models fixture swaps, tooling offset re-calibration, and optical lap changes.
  • CP-SAT Circuit Modeling: Enforces transition times between consecutive operations on the same machine.
  • Intelligent Clustering: Solvers cluster similar product families together to minimize total setup downtime.
Slide 8: In-Planning Phase 2 — Manual Material Handling & Buffers SLIDE 08

Manual Cart / Trolley Inter-Bay Transit

  • Tailored to Factory Reality: Eliminates robotic AGV complexity; models real manual trolley and cart transit.
  • Transfer Delays (τab): 0 mins (same machine), 5–10 mins (same bay), 15–30 mins (inter-department move).
  • Physical Operations Included: Deburring, part washing, packing into tote bins, and rolling the cart to the next cell.
  • Precedence Constraint: Start(Op_next) ≥ End(Op_current) + τab.

Workstation Staging Buffers & Blocking

  • Finite Intermediate Storage (FIS): Each cell staging area has a physical limit (e.g. 2–3 carts).
  • Blocking Job Shop Rule: If downstream buffer is full, the upstream machine cannot unload. The machine is blocked, protecting aisles from congestion.
  • Cumulative Constraints: Managed via CP-SAT cumulative intervals over staging capacities.
Slide 9: In-Planning Phase 3 — Process Batching & Thermal Time Lags SLIDE 09

Coating Chamber Batching [Bmin, Bmax]

  • Chamber Economics: Running single parts in vacuum coating chambers is economically infeasible.
  • Lot Sizing Bounds: Enforces minimum chamber fill (e.g. ≥ 20 units) and maximum volume (≤ 100 units).
  • Multi-Order Batching: Groups parts from different customer orders sharing identical optical coating recipes.
  • Backward Locking: Polishing tasks are pulled backward to hit the exact scheduled vacuum pump-down window.

Zero-Wait (ZW) & Curing Time Lags

  • Zero-Wait (ZW): Prevents freshly coated glass from oxidizing before protective sealing.
  • Limited-Wait (LW): θmin ≤ Start(Op_next) - End(Op_batch) ≤ θmax.
  • Cooling & Shelf-Life: Parts must cool for at least θmin (e.g. 60 mins) and enter assembly before adhesive shelf-life expires (θmax).
Slide 10: In-Planning Phase 4 — Human-in-the-Loop Disruption Repair SLIDE 10

Robust Safety Buffering (No Live IoT Needed)

  • Brownfield Grounding: Precision CNCs and polishers lack streaming sensors; schedules cannot be minute-brittle.
  • Protective Time Slacks: 5–10% safety buffer added to bottleneck machine durations.
  • Variance Absorption: Absorbs minor manual clamping or tool adjustment slips without breaking downstream schedules.

Interactive Supervisor Disruption Console

  • UI Event Logging: Supervisor logs machine downtime or job delay in Streamlit dropdowns.
  • Mode 1 — One-Click Right-Shift Repair: Pushes downstream tasks forward without changing machine sequence or assignments. Zero floor confusion!
  • Mode 2 — Local CP-SAT Re-Optimization: Locks completed/in-progress tasks and re-routes unstarted tasks across remaining parallel machines.
